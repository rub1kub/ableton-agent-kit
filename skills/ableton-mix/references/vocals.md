# Vocal editing, tuning and balance

Begin with the user's preferred section as the reference for confidence, tone and relative level. Preserve it before experiments. A louder, darker or more compressed verse can feel more serious than a playful hook even if meters look acceptable.

## Edit before processing

Keep the full take intact and muted on an archive track. Work from copies or independent arrangement clips referencing the same dry source. Separate verse/hook/intro/outro by actual performance and musical position, not by filenames or assumed take order. Keep replacement takes identifiable.

Align stressed syllables against kick/snare/synth gestures. Decide whether the performance should lead, sit on or follow the beat. Move phrases first; move syllables only where the improvement is clear. Preserve articulation and intentional push/pull. Crossfade discontinuities, but do not erase plosive attacks that make a word understandable.

Measure individual phrases before large compression changes. Bring down isolated shouts and recover genuinely lost endings with restrained clip/phrase gain. Do not equalize every word's RMS. Breath, punctuation and dynamics are part of delivery. If the beat becomes quieter, a fixed vocal fader may become too dominant; automate the relationship over that section.

## Tuning by musical role

Confirm key candidates with bass/melody over each phrase. Stable bass notes are harmonic clues, not a complete chord transcription. User-provided key remains a hypothesis until checked against the actual audio. Work on voiced vowels/endings with credible F0; noisy consonants and whispers are poor tuning targets.

Use available native Auto Shift or an already installed suitable processor. Discover current parameters and ranges; do not reuse indices from a different Live version. Auto Shift's MIDI pitch input can target selected notes, but only claim note-directed tuning after the MIDI routing and resulting pitches are verified. Correct key selection alone does not prove every phrase is in tune.

For intentional hard-tune moments, shorten response on selected voiced endings and test octave transitions. Preserve intelligibility during fast rap; automate treatment by section or word. Formant and pitch are separate dimensions. A whole word should not change from untreated to octave-up halfway through unless that is the requested effect.

If the user loves an existing high-register ending, capture/reuse that approved wet texture or duplicate the exact processing context. A raw +12-semitone copy can have completely different timing, formants and transients. Preserve the original approved occurrence while adding variations.

## Tone and dynamics

Use low cuts to resolve demonstrated rumble or masking, not to remove the voice's identity. Choose the cutoff in context of bass and vocal register. A request for “less bass in my voice” applies to vocal tracks/returns, not automatically the instrumental.

Control harshness dynamically when it occurs only on some syllables. Deep permanent midrange cuts can weaken diction. De-ess after considering pitch correction, compression and saturation, since those can expose sibilants. Inspect consonants in context before suppressing them.

One compressor need not do every job. A restrained peak stage plus slower phrase control, or a quiet parallel density layer, may help; add a stage only when it solves a measured/heard problem. Match output loudness when evaluating. Compression cannot replace an uncertain performance, and more processing may accentuate it.

Keep saturation optional and level matched. Phone/bandpass color is a section effect, not an excuse for low intelligibility across the whole track. Preserve an intelligible center when experimenting with wide motion, chorus or formant layers.

## Balance against a stereo beat

The stereo beat cannot give independent kick/bass control without stems or separation artifacts. If masking is local, use subtle dynamic spectral space or section gain on the beat; inspect that the favored synth character survives. Do not label a simple broad cut as stem-level mixing.

Judge lead, wet returns and doubles together. A wet layer can be audible alone and disappear in context; a loud effect can hide the next lyric. Verify the actual signal path before increasing it. For two performers, comparable perceived importance requires recordings from both, not identical fader numbers.

Finish with controlled loudness and protected punch. No master limiter setting guarantees “loud and clear.” Compare multiple gains at matched loudness, inspect clipping/oversampled peaks, and keep the dry source and editable chain.
